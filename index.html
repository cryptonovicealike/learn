Cryptocurrency Terminology

In this article, we provide you with a crypto dictionary containing some of the slang, terminology and native language used in the cryptocurrency space that every crypto novice should know.

Node

A computer or device that participates in a blockchain network, storing data, verifying transactions, and maintaining network security.

Altcoin

Short for "alternative coin." Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. Ethereum, Solana, and Cardano are examples.

HODL

Originated from a 2013 typo of "hold." Now means "Hold On for Dear Life" — keeping crypto long-term despite price swings.

Weak Hands

Investors likely to sell when prices fall or volatility rises. Slang for those who struggle to stay invested during uncertainty.

Blockchain

A distributed digital ledger that records transactions across a network of computers, grouped into linked blocks.

FOMO

Fear Of Missing Out. The anxiety of seeing a crypto price rise rapidly and feeling pressured to buy before it's "too late."

FOBO

Fear Of a Better Option. Hesitation to commit because you worry a better opportunity might appear later.

FUD

Fear, Uncertainty, and Doubt. Negative info that creates fear around a crypto project. Not all criticism is FUD — do your research.

Shitcoin

Informal slang for a cryptocurrency considered to have little value, weak fundamentals, or poor utility. Subjective and often used as an insult.

Dead Coin

A crypto project that has effectively stopped operating or been abandoned. Consider development, community, and trading activity.

Zombie Coin

A project that appears largely inactive or abandoned but still has some market activity. Volume can be misleading on small exchanges.

Fiat Money

Government-issued currency not backed by a physical commodity like gold. Examples: South African rand (ZAR), US dollar (USD).

DeFi

Decentralized Finance — financial apps built on blockchains that operate without traditional banks. Includes lending, borrowing, and trading via smart contracts.

DeFi Degen

Slang for someone who takes highly speculative approaches in DeFi, chasing high yields without fully researching risks. From "degenerate."

Rug Pull

A scam where project creators drain funds or remove liquidity, leaving investors with losses. Always research before investing.

Pump and Dump

Market manipulation where promoters hype a low-liquidity token to inflate its price, then sell off — leaving later buyers with losses.

These are just a few of the terminologies used in the crypto space. As you continue learning, you will come across many more terms relating to blockchain technology, trading, DeFi, NFTs, wallets and digital assets.

NFTs: What Are NFTs? — Things You Need to Know About Listing Your NFTs

There are many different marketplaces and platforms in the NFT space, including OpenSea, Zora and others. It is important to understand that not every platform works in exactly the same way.

What Are NFTs?

NFT stands for Non-Fungible Token. An NFT is a unique digital asset recorded on a blockchain. NFTs can represent digital artwork, music, videos, collectibles and other types of content.

Understanding NFT Fees

One of the first things you need to understand when entering the NFT space is that creating an account on a marketplace and carrying out blockchain transactions are not necessarily the same thing.

Blockchain transactions can involve gas fees, which are payments made to blockchain validators or network participants for processing transactions. These fees change based on network conditions.

OpenSea also has its own marketplace fees. As of May 2026, OpenSea states that its typical fee for selling NFTs is 1%. Fees can change, so it is always wise to check OpenSea's current policy before listing.

Creator Earnings and Royalties

Creator earnings allow NFT creators to receive a portion of the sale price when their NFTs are sold again. However, you should not assume that setting a percentage automatically guarantees a royalty in every secondary market.

Listing Your NFTs

1

Create a Crypto Wallet

A crypto wallet allows you to manage blockchain assets and interact with decentralized applications. MetaMask is a common option.

2

Connect Your Wallet to OpenSea

Access OpenSea through a web browser and connect your wallet. Pay close attention to what you are approving.

3

Create Your NFT

OpenSea provides NFT creation tools through OpenSea Studio. You can create a collection and mint NFTs without coding.

4

Upload Your NFT

Upload your media and add information such as name, description, and supply. If you create a unique NFT with limited supply, be precise about metadata.

5

Set Your Creator Earnings

Configure the percentage you would like to receive from eligible secondary sales. Remember: creator earnings are not always guaranteed on every platform.

6

List Your NFT for Sale

Choose a fixed-price sale or another available selling format. Before confirming, carefully review the details.

Marketing Your NFTs

Creating and listing an NFT is only one part of the process. Marketing is essential if you want people to discover your work. Simply uploading an NFT and waiting for someone to buy it is unlikely to be successful.

Most importantly, always protect your wallet and your Secret Recovery Phrase. Losing access to your wallet or giving your recovery phrase to a scammer can put your assets at risk.

Let's Talk About the Ethereum Ecosystem

Ethereum is one of the most important blockchain networks in the cryptocurrency space. It is a decentralized blockchain and software platform that allows developers to build and deploy smart contracts and decentralized applications.

Its native asset, Ether (ETH), powers network activity and is used for transaction fees and staking. Ethereum's ecosystem includes DeFi, NFTs, stablecoins, wallets, L2 networks, and developer tooling.

What Are Smart Contracts?

A smart contract is a program stored on a blockchain that automatically executes rules when certain conditions are met. Smart contracts are not legal contracts in the traditional sense; they are code.

For example, a decentralized lending app could use a smart contract to automatically lock collateral and release funds when the rules are satisfied.

Ethereum vs. Bitcoin

Bitcoin came before Ethereum and was launched as a decentralized digital currency and payment network. Ethereum took a different approach by creating a general-purpose programmable blockchain.

That means Ethereum is more than just a currency. It is infrastructure for applications and digital assets.

What Makes the Ethereum Ecosystem Different?

1. Decentralized Applications (dApps)

These applications run on blockchain infrastructure and can offer services without depending entirely on centralized intermediaries.

2. Smart Contracts

Smart contracts allow developers to create programmable rules executed by the Ethereum network.

3. Tokens

Ethereum made it possible for developers to create tokens using standards such as ERC-20 and ERC-721.

4. Layer 2 Networks

Layer 2 networks scale Ethereum by processing transactions off-chain while relying on Ethereum for security and settlement.

5. Continuous Development

Ethereum has undergone multiple upgrades over the years and continues to evolve as a platform.

Let's Talk About Privacy Coins

What Are Privacy Coins?

Instead of using transparent cryptocurrencies, people can also choose cryptocurrencies that are designed to provide greater financial privacy.

Privacy coins are cryptocurrencies with technologies designed to make transaction information harder for outside observers to identify. Depending on the cryptocurrency, these technologies may mask senders, recipients, or amounts.

Which Privacy Coins Offer Strong Privacy Features?

Monero (XMR)

Monero is one of the best-known privacy-focused cryptocurrencies. It uses technologies such as ring signatures, Ring Confidential Transactions, and stealth addresses to improve privacy.

Zcash (ZEC)

Zcash is another major privacy-focused cryptocurrency. Zcash offers users the ability to make shielded transactions while also supporting transparent transactions.

Grin

Grin is an open-source cryptocurrency that focuses heavily on privacy and scalability. It uses a protocol called Mimblewimble.

Are Privacy Coins Completely Anonymous?

Privacy coins can offer more transaction privacy than transparent cryptocurrencies, but calling them completely anonymous or untraceable would be misleading. Privacy is not the same as perfect anonymity.

Final Thoughts

Privacy coins remain an important part of the cryptocurrency landscape. Understanding how they work is essential for anyone trying to understand the wider world of digital assets.
