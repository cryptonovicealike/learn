Cryptocurrency Terminology

In this article, we provide you with a crypto dictionary containing some of the slang, terminology and native language used in the cryptocurrency space that every crypto novice should know.

Node

A computer or device that participates in a blockchain network, storing data, verifying transactions, and maintaining network security.

Altcoin

Short for "alternative coin." Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. Ethereum, Solana, and Cardano are examples.

HODL

Originated from a 2013 typo of "hold." Now means "Hold On for Dear Life" — keeping crypto long-term despite price swings.

Weak Hands

Investors likely to sell when prices fall or volatility rises. Slang for those who struggle to stay invested during uncertainty.

Blockchain

A distributed digital ledger that records transactions across a network of computers, grouped into linked blocks.

FOMO

Fear Of Missing Out. The anxiety of seeing a crypto price rise rapidly and feeling pressured to buy before it's "too late."

FOBO

Fear Of a Better Option. Hesitation to commit because you worry a better opportunity might appear later.

FUD

Fear, Uncertainty, and Doubt. Negative info that creates fear around a crypto project. Not all criticism is FUD — do your research.

Shitcoin

Informal slang for a cryptocurrency considered to have little value, weak fundamentals, or poor utility. Subjective and often used as an insult.

Dead Coin

A crypto project that has effectively stopped operating or been abandoned. Consider development, community, and trading activity.

Zombie Coin

A project that appears largely inactive or abandoned but still has some market activity. Volume can be misleading on small exchanges.

Fiat Money

Government-issued currency not backed by a physical commodity like gold. Examples: South African rand (ZAR), US dollar (USD).

DeFi

Decentralized Finance — financial apps built on blockchains that operate without traditional banks. Includes lending, borrowing, and trading via smart contracts.

DeFi Degen

Slang for someone who takes highly speculative approaches in DeFi, chasing high yields without fully researching risks. From "degenerate."

Rug Pull

A scam where project creators drain funds or remove liquidity, leaving investors with losses. Always research before investing.

Pump and Dump

Market manipulation where promoters hype a low-liquidity token to inflate its price, then sell off — leaving later buyers with losses.

These are just a few of the terminologies used in the crypto space. As you continue learning, you will come across many more terms relating to blockchain technology, trading, DeFi, NFTs, wallets, and digital assets.

NFTs: What Are NFTs? — Things You Need to Know About Listing Your NFTs

There are many different marketplaces and platforms in the NFT space, including OpenSea, Zora and others. It is important to understand that not every platform works in exactly the same way.

What Are NFTs?

NFT stands for Non-Fungible Token. An NFT is a unique digital asset recorded on a blockchain. NFTs can represent digital artwork, music, videos, collectibles and other types of media.

Understanding NFT Fees

One of the first things you need to understand when entering the NFT space is that creating an account on a marketplace and carrying out blockchain transactions are not necessarily the same thing.

Blockchain transactions can involve what are known as gas fees. Gas fees are payments made to blockchain validators or network participants for processing certain transactions.

Therefore, it would be inaccurate to say that NFT gas fees always cost between $50 and $300 or that there is always a single one-time fee that allows you to list unlimited NFTs. Your actual costs depend on network conditions and the specific transaction.

OpenSea also has its own marketplace fees. As of May 2026, OpenSea states that its typical fee for selling NFTs is 1%. Fees can change, so it is always wise to check OpenSea's current terms.

Creator Earnings and Royalties

Creator earnings allow NFT creators to receive a portion of the sale price when their NFTs are sold again. However, you should not assume that setting a percentage automatically guarantees the payment will always be collected.

Listing Your NFTs

1

Create a Crypto Wallet

A crypto wallet allows you to manage blockchain assets and interact with decentralized applications. MetaMask is a common example.

2

Connect Your Wallet to OpenSea

Access OpenSea through a web browser and connect your wallet. Pay close attention to what you are approving before connecting.

3

Create Your NFT

OpenSea provides NFT creation tools through OpenSea Studio. You can create a collection and mint NFTs without coding.

4

Upload Your NFT

Upload your NFT's media and add information such as name, description, and supply. If you create a unique NFT with limited supply, be clear about that.

5

Set Your Creator Earnings

Configure the percentage you would like to receive from eligible secondary sales. Remember: creator earnings are tied to the terms of the platform.

6

List Your NFT for Sale

Choose a fixed-price sale or another available selling format. Before confirming, carefully review the details and fees.

Marketing Your NFTs

Creating and listing an NFT is only one part of the process. Marketing is essential if you want people to discover your work. Simply uploading an NFT and waiting for someone to buy it is unlikely to be enough.

Most importantly, always protect your wallet and your Secret Recovery Phrase. Losing access to your wallet or giving your recovery phrase to a scammer can put your assets at risk.

Let's Talk About the Ethereum Ecosystem

According to different cryptocurrency market-data sources, Ether (ETH), which is the native cryptocurrency of the Ethereum blockchain, is one of the largest and most widely traded crypto assets in the world.

Ethereum is one of the most important blockchain networks in the cryptocurrency space. It is a decentralized blockchain and software platform that allows developers to build and deploy smart contracts and applications.

The idea for Ethereum was proposed by Vitalik Buterin in late 2013. Several other people later became co-founders, including Gavin Wood, Joseph Lubin, Anthony Di Iorio, and Charles Hoskinson.

The purpose of Ethereum was to create a blockchain that could do more than simply transfer digital money. Ethereum was designed as a platform where developers could build applications that operate without centralized intermediaries.

What Are Smart Contracts?

One of the most important features of Ethereum is smart contracts. A smart contract is a program stored on a blockchain that automatically executes instructions when the conditions written into the code are met.

However, it is important to understand that smart contracts are not simply digital versions of traditional legal contracts. They are computer programs, and their behaviour depends on the code that defines them.

For example, imagine a decentralized lending application where a person deposits cryptocurrency as collateral and borrows another digital asset. The smart contract can contain rules determining interest rates, collateral thresholds, and liquidation conditions.

This is one of the major ideas behind decentralized finance: rules can be enforced by blockchain-based software rather than relying entirely on a traditional financial institution.

Ethereum's transactions and smart-contract operations also require gas. Gas measures the computational work required to execute operations on Ethereum, and users pay fees for that work.

Ethereum vs. Bitcoin

Bitcoin came before Ethereum and was launched as a decentralized digital currency and payment network. Ethereum took a different approach by creating a general-purpose programmable blockchain.

This does not mean that Bitcoin has no programmable functionality. Bitcoin uses a scripting system that can support different types of transaction conditions. The important difference is that Ethereum was built to support a broader range of programmable applications.

Ethereum has also continued to evolve through network upgrades. For example, the Dencun upgrade introduced data blobs designed to make it cheaper for Layer 2 networks to store and process transactions.

Another major change in Ethereum's history was its transition from proof-of-work mining to proof-of-stake in September 2022. Today, Ethereum is secured by validators who stake ETH to help validate transactions.

What Makes the Ethereum Ecosystem Different?

1. Decentralized Applications (dApps)

Ethereum provides an environment where developers can build decentralized applications. These applications can use smart contracts to perform different functions without relying entirely on a central server.

2. Smart Contracts

Smart contracts allow developers to create programmable rules executed by the Ethereum network. This is one of the biggest reasons Ethereum became more than just a cryptocurrency.

3. Tokens

Ethereum made it possible for developers to create different types of tokens using standards such as ERC-20 and ERC-721. This has helped create a huge ecosystem of assets and applications.

4. Layer 2 Networks

The Ethereum ecosystem is no longer limited to transactions taking place directly on Ethereum's main network (Layer 1). Layer 2 networks are built to process transactions using Ethereum as a settlement layer.

5. Continuous Development

Ethereum is continuously being upgraded. Unlike the idea that Ethereum simply launched in 2015 and has remained unchanged, the network has gone through numerous upgrades over the years.

Examples of Projects in the Ethereum Ecosystem

  • Golem — A decentralized computing network designed to allow users to share and access computing resources.
  • Uniswap — A decentralized cryptocurrency exchange that allows users to trade digital assets through smart contracts.
  • Chainlink — A decentralized oracle network designed to connect blockchain-based smart contracts with data from outside the blockchain.
  • Aragon — A project focused on tools for creating and managing decentralized organizations.

Ethereum vs. Ether (ETH)

This is where many cryptocurrency beginners become confused. Ethereum refers to the blockchain network and its broader ecosystem. Ether (ETH) is the native cryptocurrency used on that network.

ETH is used for several purposes within the Ethereum ecosystem, including paying transaction and computation fees. ETH can also be staked by validators as part of Ethereum's proof-of-stake security model.

A lot of people in the cryptocurrency space use the words "Ethereum" and "ETH" interchangeably during casual conversations. Technically, however, they are not the same thing.

The Ethereum Ecosystem Today

Today, Ethereum supports a broad ecosystem involving smart contracts, decentralized finance, NFTs, stablecoins, wallets, Layer 2 networks, oracles, decentralized applications, developers, and users.

That is one of the most interesting things about Ethereum: it is not simply a cryptocurrency. It is a blockchain platform that provides infrastructure on which other applications and digital assets can be built.

Let's Talk About Privacy Coins

What Are Privacy Coins?

Instead of using transparent cryptocurrencies, people can also choose cryptocurrencies that are designed to provide greater financial privacy.

Privacy coins are cryptocurrencies with technologies designed to make transaction information more difficult for outside observers to identify. Depending on the cryptocurrency, these technologies can hide sender, receiver, or amount details.

However, privacy does not necessarily mean complete anonymity. The level of privacy depends on the cryptocurrency, the technology being used, and how the user interacts with the network.

Which Privacy Coins Offer Strong Privacy Features?

Monero (XMR)

Monero is one of the best-known privacy-focused cryptocurrencies. Unlike transparent blockchains such as Bitcoin, Monero is designed to make transaction details private by default.

Monero uses several privacy technologies, including ring signatures, Ring Confidential Transactions (RingCT), and stealth addresses. These technologies can significantly reduce the visibility of transaction details.

Monero has also been used by criminals, including some ransomware and darknet-market operators. However, it would be inaccurate to say that Monero is the cryptocurrency of choice for all ransomware actors.

This is an important distinction because privacy technology can have legitimate uses as well. People may want financial privacy for ordinary reasons, such as preventing strangers from viewing their transactions.

Zcash (ZEC)

Zcash is another well-known privacy-focused cryptocurrency. Zcash uses zero-knowledge cryptography to allow certain transactions to be verified without revealing all of the underlying transaction information.

Zcash gives users the ability to make transactions using its privacy features while maintaining the ability to use transparent transactions as well. This makes Zcash different from Monero, where privacy is typically on by default.

Grin

Grin is an open-source cryptocurrency that focuses heavily on privacy and scalability. Grin uses a protocol called Mimblewimble, which changes how transaction information is structured.

Grin's approach to privacy is therefore different from Monero and Zcash. Rather than simply hiding specific pieces of transaction information, Mimblewimble changes the structure of how transactions are represented.

Are Privacy Coins Completely Anonymous?

This is where things get interesting. Privacy coins can provide significantly more transaction privacy than transparent cryptocurrencies, but calling them completely anonymous or "untraceable" is often misleading.

For example, Monero's own documentation explains that its privacy technology protects transaction information, but it does not protect a user from every possible way their identity could become known.

Privacy should therefore be understood as risk reduction and information protection, rather than a guarantee of perfect anonymity.

What About Criminal Use?

Privacy coins have attracted significant attention from law-enforcement agencies, regulators, exchanges, and blockchain-analysis companies because criminals have used them in illicit activities.

However, privacy coins are not exclusively used for crime. Privacy itself can have legitimate purposes, including protecting financial information from unnecessary public exposure. At the same time, there are real risks associated with misuse.

So it would be inaccurate to describe privacy coins as simply "the easiest and safest way" to launder money.

Will Privacy Coins Be Around Forever?

That is impossible to predict. Privacy-focused cryptocurrencies face regulatory and exchange-related challenges in different parts of the world. Some cryptocurrency exchanges have removed or restricted privacy coin listings.

Blockchain intelligence companies also continue developing tools for investigating cryptocurrency transactions. One example is CipherTrace, which was acquired by Mastercard in 2021.

However, it would be misleading to say that companies such as CipherTrace are simply "trying to abolish privacy coins." Their publicly stated role is focused on cryptocurrency intelligence, security, and compliance.

The future of privacy coins will likely depend on a combination of technology, regulation, exchange support, user demand, and the continuing debate between financial privacy and financial transparency.

For now, privacy coins remain an important part of the cryptocurrency ecosystem — and understanding how they work is essential for anyone trying to understand the wider world of digital assets.

So Let's Talk About Bitcoin Pizza Day 🍕

What is Bitcoin Pizza Day?

Bitcoin Pizza Day is an annual celebration in the crypto community that takes place on May 22. It commemorates one of the most famous transactions in Bitcoin history.

On May 22, 2010, programmer Laszlo Hanyecz successfully used Bitcoin to buy two pizzas. He traded 10,000 BTC for the two pizzas, in what became the first widely known real-world commercial Bitcoin transaction.

At the time, Bitcoin was still a very young project and had almost no established market value compared with today. The 10,000 BTC were worth roughly $40–$41 at the time of the transaction.

The transaction was arranged through the BitcoinTalk forum. Hanyecz had posted an offer to pay 10,000 BTC to anyone who would get him two large pizzas. A fellow BitcoinTalk user known as "jercos" accepted the offer.

This simple pizza purchase became an important moment in Bitcoin's history because it demonstrated something that had previously been mostly theoretical: Bitcoin could actually be exchanged for real-world goods.

And yes, those two pizzas have become one of the most famous examples of how dramatically Bitcoin's value has changed. The 10,000 BTC that were worth around $41 in 2010 would be worth hundreds of millions of dollars at today's prices.

The Significance of Bitcoin Pizza Day

Real-world use

The pizza transaction showed that Bitcoin could move beyond being an idea discussed online and actually be used to purchase something in the real world. It became an early example of Bitcoin functioning as money.

An early price benchmark

The transaction also gives us an interesting look at Bitcoin's extremely early value. The 10,000 BTC were worth approximately $40–$41 at the time, meaning the transaction implied a Bitcoin price of around $0.004 to $0.0041 per coin.

A milestone for Bitcoin adoption

The pizza purchase did not single-handedly turn Bitcoin into a global financial asset, but it became an important historical milestone in Bitcoin's development. It demonstrated that people could physically spend Bitcoin in exchange for goods.

Community bonding

Bitcoin Pizza Day has also become a way for the cryptocurrency community to celebrate Bitcoin's early history. Every May 22, crypto enthusiasts remember the famous transaction and reflect on how far the network has come.

How much would the 10,000 BTC be worth today?

This is probably the most interesting part of the story. Laszlo Hanyecz spent 10,000 BTC on two pizzas in 2010. If he had somehow kept those Bitcoin instead, their value today would be enormous.

However, there is an important point to remember: the value isn't fixed. Bitcoin's price changes constantly, so the value of 10,000 BTC changes with it.

This is why Bitcoin Pizza Day often inspires what people sometimes call the "Bitcoin Pizza Index" — a fun way of calculating what those 10,000 BTC would be worth at different Bitcoin prices.

The comparison is fascinating, but we should also remember that Laszlo's transaction helped demonstrate that Bitcoin could actually be used as money. Without people being willing to spend Bitcoin, the ecosystem would not have developed as it did.

Final Thoughts

Bitcoin Pizza Day is a reminder of just how different the cryptocurrency world looked in 2010.

What started as one programmer exchanging 10,000 BTC for two pizzas became one of the most famous stories in cryptocurrency history.

Today, Bitcoin Pizza Day is celebrated every May 22 by crypto enthusiasts and serves as a reminder of Bitcoin's journey from a small experimental digital currency into a globally recognized financial asset.

So the next time you hear someone talking about Bitcoin Pizza Day, remember the two pizzas, remember Laszlo Hanyecz, and remember just how early Bitcoin really was.

RWA: Real-World Assets

RWA, or Real-World Assets, is the idea of bringing real-world assets such as real estate, government bonds, commodities, art, credit, and collectibles onto blockchain networks.

The goal is to represent these assets digitally through blockchain-based tokens, making ownership, transfer, and access more efficient and potentially more transparent. In some cases, tokenization can improve liquidity and reduce operational frictions.

Epic Chain

Epic Chain (EPIC) is a blockchain project that evolved from Ethernity Chain, a platform originally known for authenticated digital collectibles and celebrity-related digital assets.

Epic Chain's earlier post-rebrand vision included an ecosystem combining entertainment, NFTs, decentralized applications, and RWA tokenization. Its RWA ambitions included areas such as collectibles and digital ownership.

However, there is an important current update: Epic Chain's official website now presents the project primarily as a "Travel Layer for Crypto," offering crypto-based travel services.

Epic's connection with entertainment has not simply disappeared from its history. The project says that, during its Ethernity era, it worked with or advised major brands and personalities on digital collectibles and entertainment ventures.

This distinction is important because cryptocurrency projects can change direction quickly. A project can have an impressive history of partnerships while its current products and strategy look different.

RWA Tokenization

The broader idea behind RWA tokenization is straightforward.

Imagine a real-world asset being represented by a blockchain token. Instead of ownership information existing entirely inside traditional financial or legal systems, some part of that ownership could be represented digitally on-chain.

This can potentially create benefits such as:

  • Fractional ownership
  • Faster settlement
  • Greater transparency
  • Programmable ownership
  • Integration with decentralized applications
  • Access to new forms of liquidity

But there is an important catch: a blockchain token does not magically eliminate traditional financial infrastructure. Real-world assets still need legal agreements, custodians, asset managers, and compliance frameworks.

This is one of the reasons RWA projects are interesting. They attempt to connect the traditional financial system with blockchain technology rather than completely replacing traditional finance.

Be Careful With Influencer Endorsements

The involvement of celebrities, influencers, and large organizations can make a cryptocurrency project look more credible, but it should never be treated as proof that a project is a good investment.

A celebrity or influencer may have participated in a campaign, launched an NFT collection, or worked with a project without necessarily endorsing its cryptocurrency as an investment.

This is particularly important in crypto because popularity and financial value are not the same thing.

Do your own research before buying any cryptocurrency or token. Do not simply take an influencer's word at face value. Look at the project's official documentation, token economics, technology, and risk disclosures.

Ondo Finance

Ondo Finance is another major name in the RWA sector. The company was established in 2021 by Nathan Allman and Pinku Surana, who were previously associated with other blockchain and financial projects.

Ondo's central idea is to bring traditional financial products onto public blockchains. Its products have included tokenized exposure to U.S. Treasuries and money-market assets.

One of its best-known products is OUSG, or Ondo Short-Term U.S. Treasuries Fund. OUSG provides eligible investors with exposure to short-term U.S. Treasury-related assets.

Another Ondo product is USDY, a tokenized yield-bearing instrument backed by short-term U.S. Treasuries and other assets. Unlike simply holding the ONDO governance token, holding USDY is more directly tied to a product designed to represent real asset exposure.

OUSG

OUSG stands for Ondo Short-Term U.S. Treasuries Fund.

It is designed primarily for institutional and other eligible investors and provides exposure to short-term U.S. Treasuries and money-market funds. Ondo's current documentation lists eligibility requirements and jurisdictional limits.

This is an important distinction when discussing RWA projects. Tokenization may put an asset on a blockchain, but that does not necessarily remove securities laws, investor requirements, or geographic restrictions.

Flux Finance

Flux Finance is a decentralized lending protocol originally developed by the Ondo team and later transferred to the Ondo Foundation. It allows lending and borrowing using supported tokenized assets.

In simple terms, Flux is an example of how tokenized real-world assets can potentially interact with DeFi.

For example, supported tokenized assets can be used as collateral while users interact with lending and borrowing protocols. However, the exact assets that can be used, eligibility requirements, and associated risks depend on protocol rules and market conditions.

Traditional Finance Meets Blockchain

This is what makes Ondo Finance particularly interesting within the RWA sector.

Ondo is not trying to create a completely separate financial system with no connection to traditional finance. Instead, it combines blockchain technology with traditional financial infrastructure.

Its ecosystem has included relationships with major financial institutions and asset managers. For example, Ondo's current OUSG information commonly identifies exposure to funds managed by major firms.

This also explains why calling Ondo a completely decentralized cryptocurrency project would be misleading.

The ONDO token is associated with governance within the Ondo ecosystem, while the underlying tokenized financial products depend on legal entities, custodians, asset managers, and other traditional market participants.

There is also an important current development that should not be overlooked: Nathan Allman, Ondo's founder, died suddenly in 2026. Ondo subsequently announced that Ian De Bode would lead the business forward.

Final Thoughts

RWA is one of the more interesting areas of blockchain because it attempts to connect two worlds that have traditionally operated separately: traditional finance and decentralized technology.

Projects such as Epic Chain and Ondo Finance demonstrate different approaches to this idea. Epic has evolved from its Ethernity roots in digital collectibles and entertainment and has since expanded into travel and broader crypto services, while Ondo focuses on tokenized financial products.

However, the RWA sector is still developing, and investors should not confuse tokenization with guaranteed profits or safety.

Do your own research before buying any cryptocurrency or interacting with an RWA protocol. Understand what the token actually represents, who legally owns the underlying asset, what rights the token holder has, and how the structure operates in practice.

Blockchain technology can change how assets are represented and transferred, but it does not eliminate financial risk.
