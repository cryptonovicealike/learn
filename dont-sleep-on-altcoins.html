Don't Sleep on Altcoins

Altcoins, short for “alternative coins,” generally refer to cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin. Bitcoin was the first successful decentralized cryptocurrency, making it the original cryptocurrency and the largest name in the space.

Altcoins can have different features, purposes, and technologies from Bitcoin. Some operate on their own blockchains, while others are tokens built on existing blockchain networks. Depending on the project, altcoins can be used for smart contracts, decentralized finance (DeFi), payments, governance, gaming, NFTs, privacy-focused applications, and many other purposes.

Historically, some altcoins have significantly outperformed Bitcoin and Ethereum during particular market cycles. However, the opposite can also happen, and many altcoins carry considerably higher risks because of their volatility, liquidity, token supply, and the strength of the projects behind them.

One reason investors and traders pay attention to altcoins is the possibility of gaining exposure to different parts of the cryptocurrency ecosystem rather than concentrating entirely on Bitcoin. However, diversification does not guarantee profits or protect against losses.

Bitcoin Dominance and the Altcoin Market

The performance of altcoins can also affect Bitcoin dominance. Bitcoin dominance refers to Bitcoin's share of the total cryptocurrency market capitalization. When capital flows into altcoins and they increase in value relative to Bitcoin, Bitcoin's market share can decline.

It is important to remember that cryptocurrency market capitalization changes constantly because cryptocurrency prices can be highly volatile. Therefore, figures from previous years—such as Bitcoin's approximately $1.2 trillion market capitalization in October 2021—should not be presented as current market data.

If you want to keep track of cryptocurrency prices, trading activity, and market movements, there are several useful data tools available. One metric that can be used to study buying and selling pressure is the Money Flow Index (MFI). MFI is a technical indicator that combines price and volume to help analyze the flow of money into or out of an asset. It should not, however, be treated as a dedicated “altcoin money-flow index” or as a guarantee of future price movements.

Another useful resource is DeFiLlama, an analytics platform that provides extensive information about the decentralized finance ecosystem. DeFiLlama tracks data such as total value locked (TVL), protocol activity, chain-level TVL, stablecoins, fees, revenue, and other metrics.

One of the useful features of DeFiLlama is its Chains section, where users can compare blockchain ecosystems and examine metrics such as DeFi TVL, changes in TVL, stablecoin market capitalization, decentralized-exchange volume, fees, revenue, and active addresses.

By selecting a particular blockchain, you can also explore the protocols and applications operating within that ecosystem. For example, Ethereum has a large ecosystem containing decentralized finance applications, stablecoins, NFT-related projects, gaming applications, and many other types of blockchain projects. DeFiLlama provides data that can help users explore these ecosystems and understand where activity and capital are concentrated.

Don't Sleep on Altcoins

Bitcoin remains the original and most established cryptocurrency, but the broader crypto ecosystem has grown far beyond Bitcoin. Thousands of projects now exist, each attempting to solve different problems or create different applications using blockchain technology.

That does not mean every altcoin is a good investment. In fact, the opposite is important to understand: many crypto projects are highly speculative and can lose substantial value.

The phrase “don't put all your eggs in one basket” is often used when discussing diversification. But diversification should be based on research and an understanding of risk—not simply on the assumption that altcoins will rise whenever Bitcoin falls.

So, should you completely ignore altcoins?

I wouldn't.

Altcoins are an important part of the broader cryptocurrency ecosystem, and understanding them can help you understand where blockchain technology is developing beyond Bitcoin. Just remember that opportunity and risk exist side by side in this market.

Do your own research, understand what a project actually does, examine its ecosystem and underlying data, and never assume that past performance guarantees future results.
