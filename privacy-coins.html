Let’s Talk About Privacy Coins

What Are Privacy Coins?

Instead of using transparent cryptocurrencies, people can also choose cryptocurrencies that are designed to provide greater financial privacy.

Privacy coins are cryptocurrencies with technologies designed to make transaction information more difficult for outside observers to identify. Depending on the cryptocurrency, these technologies can hide or obscure information such as the sender, recipient, transaction amount, or transaction history.

However, privacy does not necessarily mean complete anonymity. The level of privacy depends on the cryptocurrency, the technology being used, and how the user interacts with the network.

So, which privacy coins offer strong privacy features?

Monero

Monero (XMR) is one of the best-known privacy-focused cryptocurrencies. Unlike transparent blockchains such as Bitcoin, Monero is designed to make transaction details private by default.

Monero uses several privacy technologies, including ring signatures, Ring Confidential Transactions (RingCT), and stealth addresses. These technologies are designed to hide the sender, transaction amount, and recipient respectively.

Monero has also been used by criminals, including some ransomware and darknet-market operators. However, it would be inaccurate to say that Monero is the cryptocurrency of choice for all ransomware groups or that ransomware groups no longer use Bitcoin. Recent research from TRM Labs shows that ransomware actors continue to favor Monero in some cases, while Bitcoin remains the dominant cryptocurrency for ransom payments overall.

This is an important distinction because privacy technology can have legitimate uses as well. People may want financial privacy for ordinary reasons, such as preventing strangers from viewing their transaction history or estimating how much cryptocurrency they own.

Zcash

Zcash (ZEC) is another well-known privacy-focused cryptocurrency.

Zcash uses zero-knowledge cryptography to allow certain transactions to be verified without revealing all of the underlying transaction information. Zcash therefore gives users the ability to make transactions using its privacy features while maintaining the ability to use transparent transactions as well.

This makes Zcash different from Monero, where privacy is built into transactions on the network by default. With Zcash, users can choose between transparent and shielded transactions depending on the type of transaction they are making.

Grin

Grin is an open-source cryptocurrency that focuses heavily on privacy and scalability.

Grin uses a protocol called Mimblewimble, which changes how transaction information is stored and processed on the blockchain. One of its important features is cut-through, which allows certain transaction data to be removed while still maintaining the ability to verify that the blockchain remains valid.

Grin's approach to privacy is therefore different from Monero and Zcash. Rather than simply hiding specific pieces of transaction information, Mimblewimble changes the structure of how transactions are represented and stored.

Are Privacy Coins Completely Anonymous?

This is where things get interesting.

Privacy coins can provide significantly more transaction privacy than transparent cryptocurrencies, but calling them completely anonymous or “untraceable” is too simplistic.

For example, Monero's own documentation explains that its privacy technology protects transaction information, but it does not protect a user from every possible way their identity could become known. If someone voluntarily reveals their identity, loses control of their private information, or has their device compromised, the privacy provided by the blockchain itself cannot magically undo that exposure.

Privacy should therefore be understood as risk reduction and information protection, rather than a guarantee of perfect anonymity.

What About Criminal Use?

Privacy coins have attracted significant attention from law-enforcement agencies, regulators, exchanges, and blockchain-analysis companies because criminals have used them in illicit activities.

However, privacy coins are not exclusively used for crime. Privacy itself can have legitimate purposes, including protecting financial information from unnecessary public exposure.

At the same time, the cryptocurrency crime landscape is changing. Recent research shows that criminals are not relying on one particular cryptocurrency or technique. Bitcoin, stablecoins, cross-chain transfers, exchanges, and other services can all appear in illicit financial activity.

So it would be inaccurate to describe privacy coins as simply “the easiest and safest way” to launder money.

Will Privacy Coins Be Around Forever?

That is impossible to predict.

Privacy-focused cryptocurrencies face regulatory and exchange-related challenges in different parts of the world. Some cryptocurrency exchanges have removed or restricted privacy coins because of regulatory and compliance concerns.

Blockchain intelligence companies also continue developing tools for investigating cryptocurrency transactions. One example is CipherTrace, which was acquired by Mastercard in 2021 and became part of Mastercard's broader digital-asset security and intelligence capabilities.

However, it would be misleading to say that companies such as CipherTrace are simply “trying to abolish privacy coins.” Their publicly stated role is focused on cryptocurrency intelligence, security, fraud prevention, investigations, and compliance.

The future of privacy coins will likely depend on a combination of technology, regulation, exchange support, user demand, and the continuing debate between financial privacy and financial transparency.

For now, privacy coins remain an important part of the cryptocurrency ecosystem—and understanding how they work is essential for anyone trying to understand the wider world of digital assets.
