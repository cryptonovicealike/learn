Crypto Mining Pools: Understanding How They Work

Crypto mining is a highly competitive venture, which has made individual mining increasingly expensive, risky and less predictable in terms of profitability. Solo mining may not be a realistic option for many crypto novices, which is why some miners consider an alternative: joining a crypto mining pool.

Individual Mining, Also Known as Solo Mining

Solo mining refers to mining cryptocurrency independently rather than combining computing power with other miners.

The main appeal of solo mining is that miners get to keep the rewards they earn without sharing them with other participants.

A solo miner uses their own resources to mine Bitcoin or other proof-of-work cryptocurrencies. These resources may include personal capital, specialised mining hardware, compatible software, computing power and electricity.

Solo miners operate their equipment independently. If they successfully mine a valid block, they receive the block subsidy and transaction fees associated with that block, subject to the network's rules.

However, cryptocurrency mining has become increasingly competitive. The substantial computing power required to compete makes solo mining more expensive and risky, particularly for individuals with limited resources.

Consequently, solo mining may not be a practical option for many beginners. An alternative is to join a mining pool.

Crypto Mining Pools

A crypto mining pool is a collective of individual miners who combine their computational power to increase their collective chances of successfully mining new blocks.

Instead of competing independently, participants contribute their computing power to a shared mining effort. The pool tracks each participant's contribution and distributes eligible mining rewards according to its payment method.

Mining pools do not necessarily require miners to physically combine their equipment. Participants can operate their own machines from different locations while connecting them to the same pool through mining software and an internet connection.

The main advantage of joining a mining pool is that participants generally receive smaller but more frequent payouts than they would through solo mining. This reduces the uncertainty associated with waiting for an individual machine to discover a block. Actual payout frequency still depends on the pool's payment method, payout threshold and rules.

The downside, however, is that rewards are shared among participants rather than being kept entirely by one miner. Pools also charge fees for their services, depending on their payment arrangements.

It is important to understand that joining a mining pool does not, by itself, increase the total computing power of the Bitcoin network. Instead, it combines participating miners' resources into a coordinated mining effort and distributes rewards according to their contributions.

Reward Distribution Methods in Mining Pools

Mining pools use different reward distribution methods to determine how participants receive their earnings.

Since participants may contribute different amounts of computing power, rewards are generally calculated according to their accepted mining work and the pool's specific payment rules.

Pool operators manage the process of recording shares, calculating contributions and distributing eligible rewards.

Before understanding these methods, it is important to understand two basic mining concepts:

  • Mining shares: A share is proof that a miner has completed a certain amount of computational work. Mining pools use shares to measure participants' contributions. A share is not necessarily a valid Bitcoin block hash; it usually meets a lower difficulty target established by the pool.
  • Block rewards: In Bitcoin mining, the total reward associated with a successfully mined block consists of the block subsidy and the transaction fees included in that block.

Some commonly used reward distribution methods include:

1. PPS (Pay Per Share)

PPS stands for Pay Per Share.

It is a reward distribution method in which miners receive a calculated payout for every valid share they submit to the mining pool, regardless of whether the pool successfully mines a block during that period.

The payout is calculated using the share's difficulty and the pool's applicable reward formula, minus any relevant pool fees.

You can compare this method to paying an employee a regular salary. The employee receives payment for their work rather than having their salary depend on whether the company makes a profit on a particular day.

The obvious advantage of this method is income predictability. Miners can estimate their earnings based on the number and difficulty of their accepted shares.

However, PPS pools generally charge higher fees because the pool operator assumes the financial risk associated with paying miners even when the pool has not successfully mined a block.

2. PPS+ (Pay Per Share Plus)

PPS+ stands for Pay Per Share Plus.

It is an enhanced version of the traditional PPS method. Under a common PPS+ arrangement, miners receive PPS-style payments for the block subsidy, while transaction fees are distributed separately, commonly using the PPLNS method.

This means that miners receive compensation for their accepted shares under the PPS arrangement, while their share of transaction fees is calculated according to their contribution within the relevant PPLNS window.

The advantage is that miners can receive relatively predictable payments for the block subsidy while also participating in the transaction-fee earnings generated by successfully mined blocks.

However, the transaction-fee component can fluctuate depending on the number and value of fees included in the blocks mined by the pool.

It is worth noting that the exact implementation of PPS+ can differ between mining pools. Miners should always consult their chosen pool's payment rules.

3. PPLNS (Pay Per Last N Shares)

PPLNS stands for Pay Per Last N Shares.

It is a reward distribution method in which miners receive a portion of the rewards when the mining pool successfully mines a block. Their payouts depend on how many eligible shares they contributed within the pool's specified window of the last N shares.

The more eligible shares a miner contributes relative to other participants within that window, the larger their proportion of the applicable reward.

Unlike PPS, PPLNS does not provide a fixed payment for every accepted share regardless of whether the pool finds a block.

Consequently, earnings can fluctuate depending on the pool's mining success and the miner's contribution during the relevant period.

PPLNS pools may charge lower fees than PPS pools, but miners must be prepared for less predictable payouts.

How to Join a Crypto Mining Pool

Joining a crypto mining pool involves several steps. Here is a beginner-friendly guide to help you understand the process.

1. Choose a Mining Pool

The first step is to research and select a reputable mining pool that supports the cryptocurrency you intend to mine.

Consider factors such as the pool's reputation, fees, payment methods, minimum payout requirements and reward distribution system before making your choice.

It is also worth checking whether the pool has servers reasonably close to your mining location, as connection quality can affect mining performance.

2. Create an Account (If Required)

Some mining pools require miners to create an account before participating, while others allow miners to connect directly without registration.

If registration is required, visit the mining pool's official website and follow its registration process.

You may need to provide an email address and create a password to access your mining dashboard.

3. Set Up Your Mining Hardware and Software

Ensure you have compatible mining hardware and software.

For Bitcoin mining, specialised hardware known as Application-Specific Integrated Circuit (ASIC) miners is generally used because of the substantial computing power required to compete on the network.

You will also need a reliable electricity supply and internet connection.

Install compatible mining software that allows your equipment to communicate with the mining pool. Some ASIC miners have mining software built into their operating systems, so separate software installation may not always be necessary.

4. Connect Your Mining Equipment to the Pool

Once your equipment and software are ready, configure your miner using the mining pool's server address, commonly provided as a Stratum URL.

You may also need to enter your worker name and password, depending on the pool's requirements.

A worker is an individual mining device or setup identified within the pool to help monitor its performance and contribution.

Follow the pool's official configuration instructions to complete the connection.

5. Configure Your Payout Wallet and Start Mining

Before receiving mining rewards, you need a compatible cryptocurrency wallet address where your earnings can be sent.

Enter this address into the mining pool's payout settings, if required.

Take note of the pool's minimum payout threshold, which is the minimum amount you must accumulate before receiving a payment, as well as its payout schedule.

Some pools also impose withdrawal fees or additional payout conditions, so it is important to understand these requirements beforehand.

Once everything is configured, start mining and monitor your computing power, accepted shares and accumulated rewards through the pool's dashboard.

Final Thoughts

Crypto mining pools provide an alternative for individuals who want to participate in cryptocurrency mining without relying entirely on their own computing power to discover a block.

By combining their computational resources, miners can participate in a shared mining effort and receive rewards according to their contributions.

However, joining a mining pool does not guarantee profitability. Hardware costs, electricity expenses, network difficulty, pool fees and cryptocurrency market conditions can all affect the financial outcome of mining.

Understanding how mining pools operate, including their different reward distribution methods, is therefore essential before committing resources to cryptocurrency mining.
