Bitcoin ATMs: Convenience, Cryptocurrency Adoption and Money Laundering Risks

Bitcoin ATMs

The invention of Bitcoin ATMs has also changed the money laundering game.

Just like depositing money into a regular bank ATM, you deposit as much money as you would like into a crypto wallet. This means that you do not need a credit card. You are also not necessarily required to provide extensive personal information to turn cash into crypto, although identification requirements vary between ATM operators.

This is great for criminals running traditional rackets. All a criminal needs to do is take their dirty fiat money and use a Bitcoin ATM to convert that dirty fiat money into Bitcoin.

That Bitcoin can eventually be turned into other coins, like privacy coins, and transferred between different wallets. Criminals may attempt to use these transactions to disguise the origins of illegally obtained money.

Convenience and cryptocurrency adoption

Bitcoin ATMs can be found in convenience stores, gas stations and malls. They have similar features to traditional ATMs. They allow customers to buy and sell cryptocurrency using cash.

Bitcoin ATMs exploded in popularity during the height of the crypto craze between 2020 and mid-2022. At the beginning of 2022, there were approximately 34,368 cryptocurrency ATMs worldwide.

Bitcoin ATMs, also known as BTMs, have attracted a surge of users, ranging from crypto novices and enthusiasts to scammers. Money launderers have also taken notice.

Bitcoin Depot was one of the major companies operating Bitcoin ATMs across the United States. Other crypto ATM operators have included CoinFlip, Athena Bitcoin and Bitstop.

The growing demand for Bitcoin ATMs is not only driven by people's interest in investing in cryptocurrency but also by their potential to serve as an alternative to slower traditional financial services.

Bitcoin ATMs have been seen as a convenient and confidential tool for both the unbanked and the underbanked, particularly in regions where access to traditional banking services is limited.

Bitcoin ATMs are not exclusive to developed economies. They have also established a presence in Africa, particularly in South Africa, where cryptocurrency ATMs have been reported in cities such as Durban, Johannesburg and Cape Town.

Many people also use Bitcoin ATMs to purchase cryptocurrency for online transactions and other digital payments.

According to research published by the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, the estimated annual revenue of the Bitcoin ATM industry in the United States was approximately $3.63 billion. This estimate was largely based on transaction fees. The research also estimated that consumers spent approximately $22.69 billion annually on Bitcoin ATM transactions.

The growing interest in Bitcoin ATMs is attracting more and more customers while also catching the attention of malicious actors.

Scams and financial crime

In 2021, experts and law enforcement officials raised concerns about scams and money laundering involving Bitcoin ATMs, as reported by CNBC.

Crypto is one of the most scam-intensive areas of finance.

This is largely due to the fact that many types of scams found in traditional finance can also be found in the crypto space. These include identity scams, impersonation scams, romance scams and many other fraudulent schemes.

The prevalence of scams and concerns surrounding financial crime have hindered the adoption of cryptocurrency as well as the establishment and growth of many crypto-related businesses.

Rising scams and regulatory pressure have also created challenges for Bitcoin ATM operators. Some companies have faced financial difficulties, lawsuits and bankruptcy proceedings.

In May 2026, Bitcoin Depot filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the United States and announced plans to wind down its operations. The company cited increasing regulatory requirements, litigation and enforcement pressures as factors that had affected its business and financial position.

Regulation and fraud prevention

In an effort to combat scams, Bitcoin ATM companies have incorporated notices on their devices in the hope of preventing users from falling victim to fraudulent schemes.

Fraud-prevention warnings can be seen on-screen, reminding users to ensure that they are sending cryptocurrency to a digital wallet they control or to an intended recipient they trust.

Despite these laws and other preventative measures, there have been cases of money laundering involving non-compliant Bitcoin ATM operators.

In 2022, Bitcoin ATM operator Robert Taylor was indicted for operating an unlicensed money transmission business in New York.

According to the Manhattan District Attorney's Office, Taylor operated Bitcoin kiosks at 46 or more locations across New York City, New Jersey and Miami. His business converted more than $5.6 million in customers' cash into Bitcoin between 2017 and 2018.

It was alleged that Robert Taylor operated discreetly and advertised the anonymity of his kiosks. His machines attracted customers who were willing to pay higher transaction fees in exchange for not having to provide identification.

In May 2024, Taylor was convicted by a New York jury on all seven counts against him, including operating an unlicensed money transmission business and criminal tax fraud.

His case demonstrates how Bitcoin ATMs can be exploited by individuals seeking to conceal the origins of illegally obtained money.

Final thoughts

Bitcoin ATMs have changed how people access cryptocurrency, offering a convenient alternative to traditional financial services, particularly for the unbanked and underbanked. However, their growing popularity has also attracted scammers and money launderers, raising concerns about fraud, financial crime and the need for stronger regulation.
